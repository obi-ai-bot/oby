# Other computers

The business behavior contract for using another computer's resources from Oby, as T3 Code's remote environments do: the owner's window on this Mac switches to another Mac's or Linux box's obid, which runs the work there. From [the template](../business-contract-template.md). Scenarios are numbered `COMPUTERS-n` (`REMOTE-n` is the factory's remote gate, [remote-gate.md](remote-gate.md)); the tests that prove them are named after them (`computers_<n>_…`, in `core/crates/obid/src/remote/`, `src/gateway.rs`, `src/main.rs` and `tests/remote.rs`), and the lab's workflows (`tools/obid-spec/workflows/lab/remote/`, `obid-spec run --only lab-remote`) are their acceptance tests against the real obid.

## Feature and status

- **Outcome:** The owner adds another computer they own, a Mac or a Linux box, and the whole window works on it: its tasks, conversations and folders, run by that computer's own obid and agents. Oby reaches it over whatever network they already have and moves between routes by itself as they come and go; when none works, it says what it tried.
- **Status:** Each scenario says whether it's implemented. **Implemented:** obid's pieces (`core/crates/obid/src/remote/`): routes and their order, the pin, the link, the supervisor's decisions, SSH as a tunnel and what its errors mean, `obid pair-computer`, the gateway served over HTTPS with this computer's own certificate, the pinned client, the tunnel, and pairing with a code, a computer's own kind of pairing (the gateway's list, its own key's words, removed with its own token), and the Mac keeping its computers for the window, adding one with a code or over SSH and sending the window's requests that name it there (COMPUTERS-17 to 25). **Proposed:** the scenarios marked so. The owner's decisions: [2026-11-19-other-computers.md](../decisions/2026-11-19-other-computers.md).
- **Scope:** Macs and Linux. **Out of scope:** the Obysphere as the transport (never: it connects people, not one's own computers), installing anything over SSH, choosing a computer per task (later), the window's computer picker, the Electron app's: the owner approved its design (#2635), and it's built in #2662 (COMPUTERSAPP-n), on obid's computers once they're here.

## Behavior

- **Actors and ownership:** the dispatcher's ("Dispatcher, not harness"): which computer the window uses, which route reaches it, and how it pairs. The other computer's obid runs the work there as it would for its own window; nothing an agent does changes. The person's own `ssh` (their configuration, keys, agent and `known_hosts`) is the only SSH Oby uses.
- **Triggers and prerequisites:** the other computer runs obid with a `gateway` (an address on its own 127.0.0.1). Over SSH, the person can already reach it with their own key. Without SSH, the other computer's owner is at it to make a code ([why](../decisions/2026-11-19-other-computers.md)).
- **Routes.** A route is a direct address or an SSH alias. A direct address is always HTTPS with the other computer's certificate pinned by its SHA-256: a plain address can belong to another machine after a network change, and only the pin says who answers. An SSH route is a tunnel (`ssh -N -L`) from this Mac's 127.0.0.1 to the other obid's gateway on its own 127.0.0.1, and nothing else. Routes go in the owner's order: the same machine, the LAN, a VPN or tailnet, public, then SSH; Tailscale is one tailnet among others, never required ([why](../decisions/2026-11-19-other-computers.md)).
- **The supervisor.** The window uses the best route that answers. It moves down on the current route's first failure, and up only once a better route answered twice in a row. With none answering it says so once, naming every route it tried and why.
- **Adding over SSH.** On the owner's click, the Mac runs the other computer's `obid pair-computer` over their SSH with its name and device key on stdin. That names the key in obid's `home/pairing`, registers it there and makes a one-time invitation; the Mac redeems it through the tunnel for a device token and signs the owner's words there with its own key.
- **Adding with a code.** The other computer's owner makes a six-digit code and a link holding its direct routes, each pinned; the Mac reaches a route with the pin first, then redeems the code (COMPUTERS-13).
- **Failures and recovery:** SSH's errors are said in words the person can act on (COMPUTERS-5). A host key is the person's call: Oby never accepts one or turns strict checking off. A refused pairing step leaves nothing named in `home/pairing`. A computer no route reaches is said once, with what was tried, and the supervisor keeps probing.
- **Invariants:** the Obysphere is never the way to one's own computers (COMPUTERS-8). Nothing is installed or configured over SSH: `obid pair-computer` is placed with obid. Oby never parses `~/.ssh/config` and never writes `~/.ssh`. The other obid's API token never leaves that computer; the invitation's secret goes back over SSH only; the Mac's name and key never go on a command line.

## Acceptance scenarios

- **COMPUTERS-1** (implemented). Routes rank the same machine, then the LAN, then a VPN or tailnet, then public, then SSH, keeping the person's order within a kind. An address's kind comes from what it is: private ranges, link-local, `.local`, `.lan` and `.home.arpa` are the LAN; Tailscale's ranges and `.ts.net` a tailnet; anything else public. (`computers_1_routes_rank_lan_then_tailnet_then_public_then_ssh_keeping_the_persons_order`, `computers_1_an_address_is_classified_by_what_it_is`)
- **COMPUTERS-2** (implemented). A direct address is only `https://` with a pinned certificate (SHA-256 of its DER, `sha256:<hex>` or OpenSSL's colon form), never SSH; a VPN Oby can't tell from its address is the person's to say. (`computers_2_a_direct_address_is_https_and_pinned_only`, `computers_2_a_route_is_kept_as_json_with_its_pin`, `computers_2_a_pin_reads_both_forms_and_matches_only_its_certificate`)
- **COMPUTERS-3** (implemented). The supervisor uses the best route that answers; the next takes over when it's cut; it moves up only after the better route answered twice in a row, a flap starting the count again; with none answering it says so once, naming each route tried and why; a route added later keeps the owner's order and the route in use. (`computers_3_…`, four tests; lab: LAB-REMOTE-4, `routes.json`)
- **COMPUTERS-4** (implemented). SSH is a tunnel or `obid pair-computer` only: `BatchMode=yes` (keys and the agent, never a password), `RemoteCommand=none`, `RequestTTY=no`, no ControlMaster, `ServerAliveInterval=15` × 3, `ExitOnForwardFailure=yes`, the forward from and to 127.0.0.1, the destination last after `--` and never an option, a command one plain path and plain words, no `-F` and no host-key option of Oby's. (`computers_4_…`, three tests)
- **COMPUTERS-5** (implemented). SSH's errors say what to do: a host key never accepted (check it, then `ssh <alias>` once in a terminal), a changed one (another machine may be answering: no connection), no key accepted (never a password), unreachable, a forward that failed; anything else quoted without control characters. (`computers_5_ssh_trouble_says_what_to_do`; lab: LAB-REMOTE-1 and MAC-1)
- **COMPUTERS-6** (implemented). A link is `oby-computer:` and base64url JSON, version 1, with 1 to 8 direct routes, each pinned; one with an SSH route, no route, too many, another version or anything else is refused; its name loses control characters and keeps 100. (`computers_6_…`, two tests)
- **COMPUTERS-7** (implemented). `obid pair-computer` reads the Mac's name and its device key on stdin and nothing else: anything but a P-256 key, a third line or more than 4 KiB is `That isn't a device key.`, exit 2, nothing written. With a key, it names it in `home/pairing`, registers it with that obid's own API, makes a one-time invitation for its gateway and prints `{"device", "invite"}` on one line; the Mac redeems it through its tunnel and signs the owner's words there with its key; a key it never registered is refused. (`computers_7_it_reads_a_name_and_a_device_key_and_nothing_else`; `computers_7_obid_pair_computer_pairs_the_mac_that_sent_its_key` and `computers_7_without_a_gateway_it_says_so`, in `core/crates/obid/tests/remote.rs`; lab: LAB-REMOTE-2, LAB-REMOTE-MAC-1 and 2, LAB-REMOTE-MAC2-1 and 3)
- **COMPUTERS-8** (implemented). A computer's invitation (`"computer": true`) is never through the Obysphere, even while that obid is a member of one: it's for its gateway's `url` only, and without a `url` it's `400`. (`computers_8_a_computers_invitation_never_goes_through_the_obysphere`, in `core/crates/obid/tests/remote.rs`)
- **COMPUTERS-9** (implemented). obid keeps the addresses it bound (`home/addresses.json`, 0600) each time it starts, so `obid pair-computer` reaches the obid that runs, a port the system picked included. (`computers_9_the_addresses_obid_bound_are_kept_and_read_back`; `computers_7_obid_pair_computer_pairs_the_mac_that_sent_its_key`, in `core/crates/obid/tests/remote.rs`; lab: LAB-REMOTE-MAC-2, where the gateway's port is `:0`)

- **COMPUTERS-10** (implemented). With `direct` in its configuration (addresses on any of the machine's interfaces, at most 8, and a `gateway`: [configuration.md](../api/configuration.md)), obid serves its gateway's own router over HTTPS there, nothing more: the same pairing, device tokens and allowlist, never the pairing endpoints. Its certificate is self-signed P-256, made the first time and kept in `home/keys` (0600) across starts, so its pin stays; made anew only when its files are gone or don't hold together. `GET /v1/status` names each address and its pin (`gateway.direct`); a busy address is `down` with why, never a stop. A handshake that doesn't finish in 10 seconds holds up no other. (`computers_10_a_made_certificate_is_kept_and_its_pin_stays`, `computers_10_the_certificate_is_one_rustls_serves`, `computers_10_direct_addresses_are_any_interfaces_and_need_a_gateway`; `computers_10_obid_serves_its_gateway_over_https_with_its_own_pinned_certificate`, in `core/crates/obid/tests/remote.rs`; lab: LAB-REMOTE-3, `code-pair.json`, on obid's own HTTPS)
- **COMPUTERS-11** (implemented). The window's client for a direct address trusts only the pinned certificate, and checks the handshake is signed by its key: another machine at the same address, or a new certificate on the same one, is refused, saying "another computer answered: its certificate is" and the one it showed. (`computers_11_a_certificate_other_than_the_pinned_one_is_refused`, `computers_11_the_pinned_client_reaches_its_computer_and_no_other`; `computers_10_obid_serves_…`; lab: LAB-REMOTE-7)
- **COMPUTERS-12** (implemented). An SSH tunnel is ready only once its port on this Mac takes connections (ssh binds it after it signed in); a port another program took in between is tried again on another, three times; ssh's stderr is read for as long as it runs, and what it said is why it failed; ssh runs in a process group of its own and ends when the tunnel is dropped; one that never binds is unreachable after its limit. (`computers_12_…`, two tests; lab: LAB-REMOTE-1 and 4)

- **COMPUTERS-13** (implemented). Adding with a code: `obid invite-computer`, run at the other computer by its owner, answers a six-digit code, an `oby-computer:` link holding that computer's direct addresses (every interface's own addresses for `0.0.0.0` or `::`, never loopback or IPv6 link-local, at most 8) each with its pin, and the pin's first 16 bytes to compare by eye. `POST /v1/pairing/invite` with `"computer": true` answers that link as `computerLink`. A computer's invitation lasts 10 minutes and has a slot of its own, so a phone's doesn't replace it, nor it a phone's; a wrong try counts against each open invitation and the fifth ends it; it's never redeemed through a relay. The Mac reaches one of the link's routes with its pin before it sends the code: a link pinned to another certificate sends nothing. A wrong or used code pairs nothing. The computer it pairs may add its own key there only when the invitation was made at that computer (`obid invite-computer`'s one-time `home/invite-computer`, 2 minutes) or with the owner's device's approval; with obid's API token alone it can't. (`computers_13_presence_is_once_and_only_fresh`, `computers_13_the_link_holds_the_direct_addresses_each_pinned`, `computers_13_a_computers_invitation_lasts_ten_minutes_and_a_phones_doesnt_replace_it`; `computers_13_a_computer_is_added_with_a_code_its_certificate_pinned`, in `core/crates/obid/tests/remote.rs`; lab: LAB-REMOTE-3, `code-pair.json`)

- **COMPUTERS-14** (implemented). A computer's invitation makes a computer's pairing, listed as one. Its token reaches the gateway's list, as a phone's does, through the gateway and its direct addresses, and nothing past it: never pairing, a sign-in, or a route obid's docs keep on its loopback API (the vault: "vault secrets go in only on the Mac", the owner, 2026-11-20; accounts, folders and the rest, [api/remote.md](../api/remote.md), "A computer's token"); so a computer reaches what a phone does, and the routes added to a computer's list one by one, each by name with the screen of the window that needs it, each reviewed on its own (`COMPUTER_ROUTES` in `core/crates/obid/src/gateway.rs`): `GET /v1/skills` (the message box's `/` list) and `GET /v1/connectors` (Settings › Connectors). A path that could be read two ways (an empty, `.` or `..` segment, a backslash, or an escape decoding to one of those, a `/` or a `%`) is refused before the list is read, for every token; the list reads the path decoded, and obid is sent that path encoded the one standard way, so it serves the route the list read. (`computers_14_a_path_through_the_gateway_names_one_route_as_written`; `computers_14_to_16_a_computers_token_its_own_key_and_removing_it`, in `core/crates/obid/tests/remote.rs`; `tools/obid-spec/workflows/routes/devices/delete-pairing.json`; lab: LAB-REMOTE-8, `ssh-pair.json`)
- **COMPUTERS-15** (implemented). Through a computer's token, the owner's words count only signed by the key bound to its pairing; any other key's, a registered one included, is refused before the route sees it. `obid pair-computer` names the key it registered in its invitation, with the owner's presence there, and redeeming binds it; after a code, the key the computer adds through the gateway is its pairing's. A key bound to a pairing still paired is no other's; one whose pairing ended may be. (`computers_15_a_computers_invitation_names_its_key_only_when_owned`, `computers_15_a_named_key_is_its_pairings_own_and_no_others_while_it_lasts`; `computers_14_to_16_…`; lab: LAB-REMOTE-2, LAB-REMOTE-3)
- **COMPUTERS-16** (implemented). "Remove this computer": `DELETE /v1/pairing` on the gateway with the computer's own token ends its pairing and forgets the key bound to it there, as revoking on that computer does; a phone's token can't. (`computers_14_to_16_…`; lab: LAB-REMOTE-5, `ssh-pair.json`)

- **COMPUTERS-17** (implemented). On a Mac without Remote Login, Oby says so and how to turn it on, and never changes it: ssh's `connect to host … Connection refused` is `noSshServer`, `Nothing answers SSH on {host}. If it's a Mac, turn on Remote Login there: System Settings › General › Sharing › Remote Login. Oby never changes it for you.`; a forward refused there is not it. (`computers_17_no_ssh_server_says_how_to_turn_on_remote_login`; `computers_25_…`; `post-computers-over-ssh.json`; lab item 10.)
- **COMPUTERS-18** (implemented). The window probes its route through the tunnel (obid answers there, not merely a port), and a cut route isn't redialed more than once a second: `POST /v1/computers/{id}/retry` counts a route only when obid's own status answers there, and an SSH route dialed less than a second ago that failed says why again instead of running ssh. (`computers_18_a_cut_route_is_dialed_at_most_once_a_second`; `computers_25_…`; `post-computers-over-ssh.json`; LAB-REMOTE-4.)
- **COMPUTERS-19** (implemented). The Mac adds a computer with its code, as its owner's act: `POST /v1/computers` `{"link", "code"}` with the owner's words signed by a registered device's key (unsigned, `403`); obid reaches one of the link's routes with its pin, redeems the code there, and registers the key of the device that signed there, bound to that pairing (COMPUTERS-15). A wrong code is `422` `wrong_code`, an ended one `code_ended`, one no route reaches `502` `unreachable`, and nothing is kept. Added, it's kept in `home/computers.json` (0600) with its name, `os` (from its status's `runner.os`), routes, pairing and key's id there; its device token is only in obid's vault, as `oby.computer.<id>`, taken out of every answer, log line and push. `GET /v1/computers` lists `{"current", "computers": [{"id", "name", "os", "state", "route", "tried"}]}`, never a token, and the event `computers` carries the same whenever it changes. (`computers_19_a_computer_is_kept_its_token_only_in_the_vault`; `computers_19_to_24_the_mac_adds_studio_with_a_code_and_the_window_works_there`, in `core/crates/obid/tests/remote.rs`; `tools/obid-spec/workflows/routes/devices/post-computers.json`, `get-computers.json`)
- **COMPUTERS-20** (implemented). obid reads a link for the window, which never parses one: `POST /v1/computers/link` `{"link"}` answers its name, its fingerprint (COMPUTERS-13's) and its routes; anything else is `422` `not_a_link`. (`computers_19_to_24_…`; `post-computers-link.json`)
- **COMPUTERS-21** (implemented). Which computer the window is on is kept for it: `PUT /v1/computers/current` `{"id": <id> | "this"}`, across restarts, the event `computers` after; one the Mac doesn't keep is `404` `no_such_computer`. It moves nothing by itself: the window names the computer on its requests (COMPUTERS-22). (`computers_19_…`, both; `put-computers-current.json`)
- **COMPUTERS-22** (implemented). A request to the Mac's loopback API with obid's own token that names a computer the Mac keeps in `Oby-Computer` goes to that computer, over the best of its routes that answers, pinned, with its device token, and its answer comes back as it comes, the event stream too; a request that names none, or `this`, stays the Mac's, so its agents and scripts keep working on the Mac whichever computer the window shows; `/v1/computers` and under it are always the Mac's. Only its body, content type, signed words, request key and the stream's resume point are carried, never its profile (a profile is the Mac's). Words signed by the key the Mac registered there name its id there, so they count (COMPUTERS-15). Only the window sends one: a request through the gateway, or with an agent's token, is `400` `not_the_window` and goes nowhere; a route a computer's token doesn't reach there is `404` `not_for_a_computer` on the Mac, before it leaves; an unknown computer `404` `no_such_computer`; none of its routes answering, `502` `unreachable` with every route tried and why. Its token is in no answer, event, log line or error. A forwarded event stream carries only that computer's events and ends once the window is on another computer or the Mac removed it. (`computers_19_to_24_…`, `computers_22_its_token_…`; `post-computers.json`)
- **COMPUTERS-23** (implemented). A computer is renamed on the Mac only: `PATCH /v1/computers/{id}` `{"name"}`; an empty name is `422`. (`computers_19_…`, both; `patch-computers-id.json`)
- **COMPUTERS-24** (implemented). `POST /v1/computers/{id}/retry` probes its routes now, best first, until one answers, and says its state; `computer.route` (`moved` or `lost`, with `tried`) when that changes, as when a forwarded request finds its route cut. `DELETE /v1/computers/{id}`, the owner's signed act (unsigned, `403`), ends the Mac's pairing there with its own token (COMPUTERS-16), which forgets the Mac's key there, then forgets it here, its token too, and the window back on This Mac if it was on it; a computer that doesn't answer is forgotten here all the same, and the answer says so (`"there": false`). (`computers_19_to_24_…`; `post-computers-id-retry.json`, `delete-computers-id.json`)
- **COMPUTERS-25** (implemented). The Mac adds a computer over the person's own SSH, as its owner's act: `POST /v1/computers` `{"ssh": "<host>"}` with the owner's words signed (unsigned, `403`); a host that isn't one, or could be read as an option, is `422` `not_a_host` before ssh runs. ssh, the person's own with COMPUTERS-4's options, runs `obid pair-computer` there once (COMPUTERS-7), this Mac's name and the signing device's key on its stdin only; the invitation it prints is redeemed through the tunnel to that computer's own gateway (COMPUTERS-12), and the computer is kept as COMPUTERS-19 keeps one, with the direct routes its invitation's link holds first, each pinned, and SSH last, the window starting on the best that answers. Each step is told as it happens, `computer.adding` `{"id", "step": "ssh" | "pair" | "routes", "state": "doing" | "done" | "failed"}`, a failed one with `trouble` in obid's own words (COMPUTERS-5, 17; no Oby there: `noObid`), keeping nothing. Its SSH route carries the window's requests through that tunnel (COMPUTERS-22), opened when one needs it, and removing the computer ends its tunnels. (`computers_25_the_mac_adds_studio_over_ssh_and_works_there_through_its_tunnel`, in `core/crates/obid/tests/remote.rs`; `computers_25_a_running_tunnel_is_reused_and_forgetting_its_computer_ends_it`; `tools/obid-spec/workflows/routes/devices/post-computers-over-ssh.json`; lab: LAB-REMOTE-2, MAC-2, MAC2-1 to 3)
