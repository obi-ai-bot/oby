- **On obid, few jobs fail** (the owner's decision of 2026-10-18). Like Claude Code, which ends only at a usage, turn or budget limit, a crash or a stop, and whose "success" means only that its model stopped, obid keeps `failed` for dead ends: nothing ran (the job sandbox, the repository, its clone, its worktree or branch, an attachment, its account couldn't be set up), or the agent stopped before it had a session to pick up. A job that stopped part way with a session to pick up **needs you**: it is `interrupted`, its commits kept on its branch and nothing pushed, `reason` says why and `nextStep` its one next step. That's a check that still fails after the last round (`One more round`, with `limit` set), a stall (#773: `Try again`, with `limit` and `stalled` set, the handoff note in `stalled.note`), the agent stopping mid-round (a login or crash: `Try again`, after signing in when needed; a usage limit: `resumesAt` for a five-hour one, else the grouped owner decision below), the secret scan still blocking after its fix round (`Try again` when every finding is one a round can fix, else none: a person looks at the branch), a job whose agent's last line on its last round is `GOAL NOT MET: …` while it has commits (none: its line is the `reason`, and the owner's Publish still sends it, TASKS-19), and a round whose summary starts `FAILS AS ASKED` (none: Oby doesn't publish a check that fails by itself, and today Publish by hand is refused too; tell it what you want. - `stoppedBy` (obid only, add-only): on a `stopped` job, who stopped it and why, in obid's words, never shown as they are (`Stopped through Oby's API`, `Stopped by a conversation`, `Stopped: its pull request was merged or closed`, …); a stop the owner asked for (`POST /v1/jobs/{id}/stop`, `POST /v1/stop-all`, a conversation's) that came while the job waited on them for what they were shown (its agent's question or permission, a yes to a login) ends `while its question waited`, and only that stop gets a failure record (docs/contracts/tasks.md, TASKS-18). A passing error on an account its usage or the tool says is full is a usage limit (`resumesAt` or `accountLimit`), not tried again. `step` says `Trying again in <time> (<n> of 3)`, counting down by the minute, with the real error after it only on the third (`Trying again in 15 minutes (3 of 3): API Error: 529`). The 3 tries are the job's, kept with it, and count cuts in a row: a turn that ends with a result, or one whose run moved the worktree's head, gives them back, as the owner's own words do (signed, their Confirm, or a turn of a conversation they're watching); a restart gives none. Over all its automatic continuations (these, RECOVERY-3/4's, a first fetch's or launch's (RECOVERY-14), every job at work an unclean restart picks up, and those after obid's own bug), obid carries a job on by itself at most 10 times; at the 10th it's `interrupted`, `nextStep` `Try again`, its branch kept, `reason` `Oby carried this on by itself 10 times, so it left it here. The last time: <error>. Try again carries it on.` Only the owner's words give them back (RECOVERY-17). Past them it ends as any other error does: `interrupted`, `nextStep` `Try again`, the real reason. Stop works during a wait, words for the job end it (they go with its next try), and a waiting job keeps its slot (docs/contracts/admission.md, ADMISSION-4). A try on a tool that can't load its session (a Cursor without `loadSession`) leaves the job `interrupted`, `Cursor can't load its session…`, never a new session told to carry on. While Claude Code retries an API call by itself (`system` `api_retry`), `step` says the same with its own numbers, `Trying again in 8 seconds (1 of 10)`, then `Working` once it's heard from again.
  - **The network before a session (docs/contracts/recovery.md, RECOVERY-14):** a job's first clone or fetch that fails on git's own words Publish would try again on, never the repository's name or address, (`could not resolve host`, a refused, reset or timed-out connection, `early eof`, a 429 or 5xx), or its CLI that ends before the job has a session, with no result and its last line the network's (`Connection error`, `Request timed out`, a 5xx or 429; never on an account the tool or its usage says is full), stays `queued`, keeping its slot (ADMISSION-4), with `step` `Trying again in <time> (<n> of 3)`, and tries again after 30 seconds, 2 and 5 minutes (never longer than `stallAfter`); Stop ends the wait for good. Past them it's `failed` with the real reason. Each try is one of the 10 continuations a job gets in all (RECOVERY-17): with none left it's `interrupted` with the cap's reason and `nextStep` `Try again`, not tried again. An owner's job that fails anywhere else while it has a `session` ends `interrupted` with `nextStep` `Try again`, never `failed`.

### `POST /v1/jobs`

Starts a job described whole, for clients that know the work already (oby-mcp, scripts); what the user says starts through `POST /v1/plans`. On obid, a job with no `conversation` that follows nothing up starts in a conversation of its own, named after it: its `task` as the owner's words (marked as no app of the owner's signed them when unsigned), its card under them, and Oby's line saying it started (`On it. {"job": {"repo": "deskree-inc/oby", "kind": "fix", "task": "Fix the login screen's empty state (#12).", "issue": 12,

- `repo` (a known repository; on obid, one its configuration lists or one of GitHub's that gh can push to: [obid only: repositories](#obid-only-repositories)), `kind` (`review`, `fix`, `build`, `test`, `investigate` or `research`) and `task` (1 to 2,000 characters) are required.
- `pr` or `issue` says what it's about; both only for an issue's work started from a pull request's head (a `fix`, `build` or `test`). `title` is 1 to 60 characters, one line. On obid, a `review` with a `pr` reads the pull request's head as obid fetches it when the review starts, its `startCommit`, never a branch of that name obid's clone kept (merging.md MERGING-37); it runs no check, whatever `check` says, and takes no check turn, its pipeline has no `check` stage, and its agent is told obid runs the check, not the review (MERGING-36).
- `account`: an account by id or name, or `auto` (or left out) for routing (Routing, under Account); `agent` (`claude`, `codex` or `cursor`) keeps routing to one tool. The job runs on the account named, or on the one its `startWhen` waits on.
- `branch` (a name git accepts; left out, Oby names one, like `oby/fix-12`; a new one the job publishes by itself as it does an `oby/` one; one the remote or obid's clone already has, its job sends only on the owner's signed Publish with `to`, `anyway` or `force` (PUBLISHING-9 and 10); `HEAD` and names starting `refs/`, `heads/`, `remotes/`, `tags/` or `origin/` are `422`, PUBLISHING-13), `base` (left out, the repository's default branch), `rounds` (1 to 10, or to the owner's `limits.rounds`; left out, 3 for a `fix`, `build` or `test` job with a check, else 1), `check` (one line of up to 200 characters), `model` (a model of the job's tool, like `claude-fable-5-1`, or a provider's id, like Bedrock's `us.anthropic.claude-…-v1:0` or Vertex's `claude-…@…`: letters, digits and `-._:@/[]`, never a leading `-`; left out, Settings › Models), `files` (up to 30 paths inside the repository; `[]` claims none; left out, the issue's), `reads` (research only: up to five other known repositories; unknown, duplicate or same-as-job repositories return `422`), `startWhen` (Start when, above; left out or `[]`, it starts at once), `request` (the user's own words; left out, the task), `clientRequestId` (obid only: a request key, below), `mode` (`ask`, `acceptEdits`, `plan` or `bypass`, Permission modes below; left out, Settings › Permissions' default; anything else is `422`). Research runs at complex effort for two to four rounds. A job the brain plans gets the mode the user named ("plan first") the same way, else the default.

Returns the queued `Job` (`201`), routed and claimed as a planned one, as a `job` event too. `422` with why when something's wrong: an unknown repository or kind, an empty task, an account that isn't signed in, a path outside the repository, a model the tool doesn't have, or `startWhen` that can't be (Start when). On obid, also while a queued or running job on the same repository works on the branch it would take, letter case aside (two jobs on one issue both take `oby/fix-34`, or the `branch` it names): `<title> (job <id>) is working on oby/fix-34 now: tell that one instead.`, so no two jobs ever move one branch (docs/contracts/publishing.md, PUBLISHING-4). **A request key** (obid only, R02-04; docs/contracts/receipts.md, RECEIPTS-5 to 7): a client that may send the same request again (a retry after a lost answer) names it with a key of 1 to 128 characters, as `job.clientRequestId` or an `Idempotency-Key` header (visible ASCII, sent once); they mean the same, and the gateway forwards the header. `422`, and nothing made, for a `clientRequestId` that isn't a string, a key that's empty or longer than 128 characters, a header that isn't visible ASCII or comes more than once, or both keys sent and different. `"clientRequestId": null` is none. The same body again with that key, within 24 hours, answers `200` with that job as it is now (the receipt's answer if obid no longer has the job) and makes nothing; the key may come in the header one time and in the body the next. ### `GET /v1/jobs/{id}/transcript` (obid only)

With no `after`, every entry. A client that reconnects asks with the highest `n` it has and updates by `n`, so it never shows one twice, and two clients see the same order. `?limit=<k>` (1 to 1,000, 1,000 when left out) caps how many newer entries come; earlier ones that changed always come along (they're the calls that were running). `next` is the `n` to ask `after` for the rest, or null when there's no more. `404` for a job obid doesn't have, `422` when `after` or `limit` isn't a number.

```json
 {"n": 4, "at": "2026-10-19T08:13:53Z", "kind": "plan", "summary": "1 of 2 done", "todos": [{"text": "Find it", "state": "done"}, {"text": "Fix it", "state": "running"}]},
 {"n": 5, "at": "2026-10-19T08:13:54Z", "kind": "tool", "tool": "Edit", "summary": "Editing scan.rs", "state": "done", "seconds": 0.1, "added": 3, "removed": 2},
 {"n": 6, "at": "2026-10-19T08:13:55Z", "kind": "tool", "tool": "Bash", "summary": "Running cargo test", "state": "failed", "seconds": 41.5, "exit": 101}]}
```

- `kind`: `tool`, `said` (the agent's words between calls), `thought` (its thinking: Claude Code's `thinking` blocks, Codex's reasoning summaries, Cursor's thought chunks, with `seconds` when the tool says how long), `you` (the owner's words to the job, `POST /v1/jobs/{id}/messages`), `plan` (its to-do list, whole, each time it changes: `todos`, its first 50 items, each `{text, state}` with `pending`, `running` or `done`, and `summary` "2 of 5 done" over all of them) or `start` (a round began: "Started on Codex", "Round 2 on Claude Code"; obid's own lines about a round come as `start` too: a ChatGPT account's model note, and a tool version that changed since the last round, CLIVERSIONS-6).
- A `tool` entry: `tool` is the tool's name as Claude Code says it, at most 64 characters. Codex's and Cursor's calls are named the same way: a read is `Read`, a command `Bash`, a file change `Edit`, Codex's MCP tool `mcp__<server>__<tool>`. Cursor's are named by the call's `kind` only, never its title, which the agent writes: a kind obid doesn't know is `Tool`. `summary` is the job step's words for it, at most 200 characters. `state` `running`, then `done`, `failed` or `declined` (obid or the owner refused it); `seconds` how long it ran, once it ended; `exit` a command's exit code and `added`/`removed` the lines a file change added and removed, when the tool says. A call still running when its turn ends (the job was stopped) is `failed`. Claude Code: a `tool_use` and its `tool_result`, and the to-do list from `TodoWrite`, `TaskCreate` and `TaskUpdate`. obid starts a job's Claude Code with `CLAUDE_CODE_ENABLE_TODO_TOOLS=1`: headless, it offers no to-do tool otherwise (claude 2.1.302). Every Claude Code job, in both lanes, and every own-lane Claude Code conversation also starts with `DISABLE_AUTOUPDATER=1` beside its telemetry switches (`DISABLE_TELEMETRY=1`, `DISABLE_ERROR_REPORTING=1`, PERMISSIONS-2), so it never updates itself under a run (CLIVERSIONS-5); Codex and cursor-agent document no environment switch for it (Codex's is the `config.toml` key `check_for_update_on_startup`, which obid doesn't set), so they get none. Codex: `item/started` and `item/completed`, `turn/plan/updated`. Cursor: ACP's `tool_call` and `tool_call_update`, `plan` and `cursor/update_todos`.
- A job's first round starts with its `start` entry, then a `you` entry with the job's `request`, once (a job tried again doesn't say it twice), so its session opens with what it was asked; what you tell it later comes as `you` entries too.
- **Thinking shows while it goes on.** Claude Code's stream says a thinking block started and stopped but carries none of its text. So obid writes a `thought` entry with `state` `running` and the summary `Thinking` when it starts, then writes it again as `done` with its `seconds` when it stops, or at the turn's end. When a tool gives its thinking's words (Codex, Cursor), they fill the thought just had, as long as it's the latest entry and has none yet; otherwise they make an entry of their own.
- `said`, `thought` and `you` entries carry `text`, the words whole (at most 64 KB), masked; `summary` is their first 300 characters on one line. **While the agent writes**, its words reach the `said` entry a whole line at a time: the entry comes with its first line, then each next line as a `transcript` event `{"job", "n", "append": "<text>"}` (add it to that entry's `text`), and the entry comes again whole, under the same `n`, when the message ends. A line goes out only once it's whole, since the secret scan judges whole lines. Claude Code jobs run with `--include-partial-messages` for this; Codex's message deltas and Cursor's message chunks stream the same way.
- The list and the events never carry a call's output or a file's contents; opening an entry does (`GET /v1/jobs/{id}/transcript/{n}`, below).
- **Masking**, before anything is written, in the entries and in the detail alike: every secret obid holds, plain or encoded (base64, hex, URL-encoded, over lines), becomes `[vault: NAME]` for the vault's, `[redacted: NAME]` for obid's own (a job's MCP token too, while the job lives); then each line the secret scan flags becomes `[redacted: <rule>]`, and only that line (#784, the owner's decision of 2026-10-20: before it, the whole summary was hidden). Where a secret goes on over lines, all of them: a private key from its BEGIN line through its END line (or the end), and a run of base64 or hex lines that holds a token. Text is masked before it's cut short, never after. While the agent writes, a private key not ended yet and a trailing run of base64 or hex lines are held back until the next line shows where they end, so no part of one goes out alone. A message whose held-back lines pass 64 KB stops streaming: its entry keeps what came, and the whole message replaces it when it ends (when the turn ends first, what was held is dropped).
- A `tool` entry also has `activity`, the kind its call counts as in the job's `activity` (`read`, `search`, `edit`, `command`, `test`, `tetrix`, `web`, `mcp` or `other`), and `file`, the file it reads or changes when it has one: from the worktree, or only its name when it's outside it.
- Kept in `home/jobs/<id>/transcript.jsonl` (0600), one line per change: a call that ends is written again under its `n`. obid keeps at most 5,000 entries or 8 MB of a job's transcript, and writes no new entries after that; a call already written still gets its end, so none is left `running`. A finished job's transcript, and its detail, are removed when obid starts more than 30 days after the job ended. While a job's agent runs, obid serves its transcript from memory; otherwise it reads the file.
- The transcript and its detail never reach the brain, its briefing or another Obi. A `transcript` event carries each entry as it comes or changes, and each line the agent writes: `GET /v1/events`. Both endpoints are on the gateway's allowlist.

### `GET /v1/jobs/{id}/transcript/{n}` (obid only)

The transcript's entry `n` as it is now, with its `detail` (#784): what the call took in and gave back, fetched when the entry is opened. `404` for a job or an entry obid doesn't have, `422` when `n` isn't a number.

```json
{"n": 6, "at": "2026-10-19T08:13:55Z", "kind": "tool", "tool": "Bash", "summary": "Running cargo test", "state": "failed", "seconds": 41.5, "exit": 101,
 "detail": {"input": {"command": "STAGING_KEY=[vault: staging-api-key] cargo test"},
            "output": {"text": "…\ntest scan::blocked ... FAILED\n[redacted: GitHub token]\n…", "lines": 1840, "truncated": true, "exit": 101}}}
```

- `input`: the call's arguments as the tool gave them, each string at most 64 KB: `{"command": …}`, `{"file_path": …, "offset": …, "limit": …}`, `{"pattern": …, "path": …}`, `{"query": …}`, an MCP tool's arguments. There as soon as the call starts.
- `output`, once the call ends: `text`, the last 200 lines or 64 KB of what it printed or answered, whichever is less; `lines`, how many lines it printed in all; `truncated`, whether `text` is less than all of it; `exit`, a command's exit code when the tool says. Claude Code: the `tool_result`'s text. Codex: `item/completed`'s aggregated output, an MCP tool's text. Cursor: the update's text content.
- `diff`, for a file change: `[{"path", "patch"}]`, each a unified diff (paths from the worktree, the home folder as `[home]`). Claude Code: its edit's hunks, or else the edit's own old and new text; Codex: its file changes; Cursor: its diffs.
- Masked as the transcript is (above). `said`, `thought`, `you`, `plan` and `start` entries have no `detail`: their `text` is all there is.
- Kept in `home/jobs/<id>/transcript-detail.jsonl` (0600), at most 32 MB a job; past that, a call's entry keeps its summary and its detail is `{"kept": false}`. Deleting a job removes its transcript and detail too.

### `POST /v1/jobs/{id}/stop`

Stops a queued or running job. Returns the job (`status: "stopped"`).

**A request key** (obid only, R02-05; docs/contracts/receipts.md, RECEIPTS-11): the body is optional and only for the key, `{"clientRequestId": "…"}`, or the `Idempotency-Key` header alone (an empty body, or one that isn't a JSON object, is accepted as before). The key's rules are `POST /v1/jobs`'s (A request key, above): a bad one is `422`, and nothing is stopped. The same stop of the same job again with the key, as signed as the first (`X-Oby-Words`, which a stop gives no other weight), within 24 hours, answers the first status and the first answer as it was then, byte for byte, and stops nothing again (`stoppedBy` stays the first one's). The key used for another request, or for this one signed differently (a retry whose signed words were already used: sign it again), is `409` (RECEIPTS-15). A `404` keeps no receipt. With no key, nothing here applies.

### `POST /v1/stop-all`

Stop all (⌃⌥⌘. or the menu on the Mac, Stop all on the iPhone): every queued and running job stops at once, as `POST /v1/jobs/{id}/stop` does for one. Each agent is asked to stop and ended within 2 seconds, with everything it started; a job that was publishing sends nothing more. No body. Answers with how many it stopped and those jobs, now `stopped`:

```json
{"stopped": 2, "jobs": [{"id": "9f08fd10", "status": "stopped", …}, …]}
```

With nothing running: `{"stopped": 0, "jobs": []}`.

### `POST /v1/jobs/{id}/dismiss`

Lets go of a job that needs you (`done` with commits it couldn't publish, `failed` or `interrupted`): it stays as it ended, in the list and in history, and needs nothing. Its alert clears (the runner sends the clearing push below), Home stops counting it, and Publish isn't offered. Returns the job with `dismissed` set. A job dismissed already comes back as it is. No device signature: nothing goes out, but GitHub's auto-merge obid armed on its pull request is turned off at once (`autoMergeOff`, MERGING-41), as it is when the job is let go any other way (Dismiss on an inbox item of its, a Confirm that dismisses it).

`404` for an unknown job. `409` when the job doesn't need you (running, stopped, done and published, or done with nothing to publish).

### `POST /v1/jobs/{id}/archive` (obid only)

Archives a job that ended and needs nothing, or unarchives one: `{"archived": true}` or `{"archived": false}` (no body is `true`). Returns the job with `archived` set (or null), adds a line to its `changes`, and sends its `job` event. Through the gateway too. Archiving one archived already, or unarchiving one that isn't, returns it as it is. `404` for an unknown job; `409` for a job that's queued or running, or one that needs you (`Dismiss it first, or publish it.`); `422` for another body. Nothing goes out.

### `PATCH /v1/jobs/{id}` (obid only)

Renames a job, whatever it's doing: `{"title": "Vault tests"}`. `{"runsOn": null}` (alone or with `title`) is taken and changes nothing; any other `runsOn` is `422` (`Jobs run only on this Mac now: runsOn is null or left out.`), as it is on `POST /v1/jobs`. The title is trimmed: one line of 1 to 60 characters, as a new job's `title`, with no line break (`\n`, `\r`, U+2028, U+2029) or other control character. Returns the job with its new `title`, adds `Renamed by you` to its `changes`, and sends its `job` event. The apps rename in place (Sangam's Rename, in the task's menu and the title bar's). `422` for a title that breaks those rules; `400` for a body that isn't that JSON (another key, a `title` that isn't a string); `404` for an unknown job. Through the gateway too. Nothing goes out.

### `DELETE /v1/jobs/{id}` (obid only)

Deletes a job that ended, for good: its record, its worktree (`git worktree remove --force`, then the folder) and its local branch in obid's clone, unless obid didn't make that branch (`oby/…` ones are obid's, and a new one the owner named, PUBLISHING-13: a pull request's head that a review fetched stays) or it's shared: another job on the same repository names it, whatever its status (a follow-up on its first job's branch, repeat jobs on one issue or pull request, a review of a pull request's head), or it's the base or the default branch; then the branch stays. The check and the removal happen under one lock, so nothing starts, publishes or takes the branch up meanwhile. Nothing pushed is touched: a pushed branch and its pull request stay on GitHub. Its open inbox items are done, with no answer. Answers `204`, and every app following events gets `job` `{"id": "<id>", "deleted": true}` and drops it. `404` for an unknown job; `409` for a job that's queued or running, being published, with its pull request still open (close or merge it first, or archive the job), or whose follow-up carries its branch on. Through the gateway too. The apps ask the owner first (Sangam's Delete task); a conversation's `oby_delete_job` always waits for the owner's Confirm.

### `POST /v1/jobs/{id}/delete` (obid only)

The apps' Delete task (Sangam's TaskMenu; the owner's decision of 2026-10-30: ask first only when something would be lost). A job with no commits that aren't published (the job's `unpublished` count, as its `snapshot` has it) and no work that isn't committed (obid's own `wip:` commit at its worktree's HEAD, or changes in the worktree, as git sees them in the job's sandbox; asked too when git can't say) is deleted at once, exactly as `DELETE /v1/jobs/{id}` does, and answers `{"deleted": true, "ask": null}`. One with commits that aren't published, or with such work (TASKS-11: its title then ends `; work that isn't committed is lost)`), isn't deleted: obid opens the same `delete_job` Confirm item `oby_delete_job` does, titled the same way (`Delete Vault tests (its worktree and local branch go; 2 commits that aren't published are lost)`), with the job's `snapshot` in its payload, in place of any such item still open for the job, and answers `{"deleted": false, "ask": <the inbox item>}`. Only the owner's signed Confirm of that item (`POST /v1/inbox/{id}/answer`) deletes the job, and a Confirm after another round or commit is refused (`409`), as for every `delete_job`. `404` for an unknown job; `409` for one obid wouldn't delete (the same words as `DELETE /v1/jobs/{id}`), with no item. No body. Not through the gateway: a paired phone deletes with `DELETE /v1/jobs/{id}`. Nothing goes out.

### `POST /v1/jobs/{id}/messages`

Tells the job's agent something:

```json
{"text": "Also add a test for the empty case."}
```

- **Running:** the agent hears it now, in the turn under way or as the next one (Claude Code: another message; Codex: it steers the turn). On obid, a turn only these words started, after the agent's work turn ended, adds its reply to `summaries` and leaves `summary`'s first line (what the card and the alert show) to the work (#699); when the owner's own words were among them, its reply is added after the work's report in `summary`, starting `After what you told it: ` (#1661, TASKCONV-14).
- **Not started yet, on obid** (`queued`, or `running` while it gets ready, #930): the words are kept (`notes`) and its first prompt ends with them, as with words the job had when it was made. `step` keeps saying what it waits for (`Waiting for a free slot: 6 of 6 jobs running`). A job stopped before it starts drops them; `notes` keeps them. One that ends before its first prompt took them (it failed getting ready) treats the owner's own as words told while it wrapped up (below), kept with the job across a restart; words that weren't the owner's own start nothing (#1661, TASKCONV-15).
- **Wrapping up, on obid** (its last turn is over: its check, the decision check, publishing; or its check is running): the owner's own words are kept (`200` with the job). A round obid opens after that (a rebase, a commit, the decision check, the secret scan's fix, a passing check's next turn) ends with them; if the job ends instead (a crash's `failed` too), they start its follow-up once it has ended, as if the owner sent them then (Finished, below): the first is its `task` and `request`, and the others end its first prompt, each as it was sent, so none is cut by the 2,000 characters; with a follow-up working already, each goes to that one. They leave the job only once taken. Refused (its safety limit, or a follow-up that can't take them yet), they stay with it, and a `confirm` item, `Start a follow-up of <title>` (`message_job`), tries again with them; its next follow-up takes them in any case. A job the owner stopped meanwhile drops them (`notes` keeps them). A follow-up that ends before it heard words it carried does the same with them, each the way it came (TASKCONV-15). Words Oby relays from a turn that read something untrusted, or from an event, are refused (`422`: `Claude Code is wrapping up: only the owner's own words wait for it now.`): only the owner's start a follow-up.
- **Finished** (`done`, `failed`, `stopped` or `interrupted`), dismissed or not, **on obid**: the words start a follow-up, as Sangam's design does (the owner's decision of 2026-10-18, which replaced picking the job up again; the frozen Swift runner still picks it up again, as under Try again below). A follow-up is a new job with the same `kind`, `agent`, `account`, `repo`, `pr`, `issue`, `base`, `check`, `model` and `conversation`, the same `mode` unless it was `bypass` (then the default mode: only the owner's own Confirm starts a job that never asks), the words as its `request` and `task`, its `title` `Follow-up: <the first job's title>` (up to 60 characters; a conversation's `oby_tell_job` may name it better, never the words themselves), and `followUpOf` the first job. Its first prompt quotes what the first job did (its title, status, request, report, branch, commits and pull request, every secret obid holds taken out), in a fence with a random word, as data. The first job stays as it ended.
  - **Where it starts.** When the first job has commits that aren't merged, the follow-up's worktree starts at the head of the first job's branch, so its agent has that work, and `startCommit` is that head. Its `commits` are what it publishes (#770), counted with git: what its branch has over the last commit any job pushed to that branch or to the branch it started from (unless that work's pull request was closed unmerged: pushed with no pull request, its commits are out), or, when none is under its head (a branch of its own, or one rebased since), over its base (its pull request's head, for a job on a pull request), the first job's commits included. So a follow-up whose agent adds nothing still ends `done` and publishes the first job's commits (after the check and the secret scan, as any job), and its pull request says which job it follows up; `Nothing committed` is only for a branch with nothing left to publish. Two exceptions publish nothing by themselves: a part of a stalled job's split counts only its own commits (its siblings carry the same ones), and a follow-up that adds none to commits whose pull request was closed without merging (that job's, or the one before the follow-ups between them that published nothing) ends `done` with them counted, unpublished, `reason` `The pull request of <title> was closed without merging, so Oby didn't publish its commits again: Publish opens a new pull request with them.`, Publish the owner's. While the first job's pull request is open (it was published, and obid hasn't read it merged or closed), the follow-up works on that very branch and publishes onto it, updating that pull request instead of opening a second (a fast-forward, or a rebase leased over the newest push along that branch's follow-up chain; someone else's divergent commit still refuses, PUBLISHING-27). Otherwise it gets a branch of its own (`oby/<kind>-<id>`), started from that head, and opens its own pull request with both jobs' commits. With no such work, it starts from its base (or the pull request's head) on a branch of its own.
  - **One at a time.** A job has one follow-up working at once, and no two jobs work on one branch: while a follow-up of it is queued or running, the words go to that follow-up instead, as if sent to it (`200` with it; `422` naming it, `<title> (<id>): …`, when it can't take them yet), never to a second one. While another job works on the branch the follow-up would take, the words are refused (`422`, naming that job: tell that one instead).
- **Try again:** the exact words `Try again, picking up where you left off. Finish with a short summary as asked.` (what Try again and `retry_job` send), or on obid those words with more after them on lines of their own (`oby_continue_job`'s), pick the agent's session up again in the same worktree and branch instead (a job moved since its last round starts a new session on its new choice: `POST /v1/jobs/{id}/move`). On obid, a job with no session (one that never started, was moved or crashed) starts a new session from its own task instead, the same job in another round, with no follow-up (TASKS-20). The job keeps its `id`, goes back to `running` and gets a new `round`; when `rounds` is above 1 it grows by one too, so a loop still has a round for this. On obid it's what an `interrupted` job's `nextStep` sends, `One more round` and `Try again` alike, and it clears `reason`, `nextStep`, `limit` and `stalled`. A dismissed job is dismissed no more. It ends as usual, except that this round may end `done` with nothing committed. obid's own Try again (the brain's `retry_job`, a `failed` item's Try again, a Confirm of `retry_job`) runs once per job `revision` (R02-06, RECEIPTS-13; docs/contracts/receipts.md): a second one for the same job and revision answers with the job as it is now and runs nothing.
- The text is saved in `notes` of the job that hears it, and `step` shows `"You: …"` until the agent moves on.
- **From a task's page** (obid only, Sangam's TaskView): `"conversation": true` puts the words, then Oby's answer, on the end of the job's `conversation`, as if said there, which the app then opens. Oby's answer is Sangam's (`tasks.ts` `relay`): `This one’s finished, so I started a follow-up. <tool> has it in <repository> on <account>.` (the follow-up's card on it, `jobs`), else `Passed to <tool>. It’ll pick that up after its current step.` (`after this round` for a loop of rounds). `"conversation": false`, or left out, adds nothing. A job whose conversation isn't kept (none, or deleted) gets one first, for it and every job of its follow-up chain, named after the chain's first job, opening with Oby's line and that job's card (docs/contracts/task-conversations.md, TASKCONV-4); the job's answer names it (`conversation`). `"attachments"`, paths in obid's own attachments folder as `POST /v1/plans` takes them (`422` naming a file outside it), go with the follow-up the words start, into its `.obi/inputs`, named between fences in its prompt; with files, `text` may be empty, and the follow-up's words are `Look at the files I attached.` (#1576); words that start none (a job at work, Try again, a follow-up already working) are `422` with them: `Files go with a follow-up of a finished task or a new task: tell this one in words.`

Returns the job that hears the words: `200` with the job itself (or its follow-up already working, obid only), or `201` with the follow-up it started (obid only). `404` for an unknown job. `422` when the text isn't 1 to 2,000 characters, or the job can't take it: wrapping up and the words aren't the owner's own (obid; the frozen Swift runner also refuses a job still getting ready or wrapping up its only turn), a review another Obi asked for, a follow-up already working (above), or, for Try again, a follow-up that carries its branch on (trying it again would move that branch under it).

**A request key** (obid only, R02-05; docs/contracts/receipts.md, RECEIPTS-8 and 9): `clientRequestId` in the body or the `Idempotency-Key` header, with `POST /v1/jobs`'s rules (A request key, above): a bad one is `422`, and nothing is heard. The same body again with the key, as signed as the first (`X-Oby-Words`), within 24 hours, answers the first status and answer as they were then (`201` with the same follow-up, or `200`): the words aren't heard again and no second follow-up starts. A follow-up the keyed words started carries the key as `clientRequestId`, in its first answer too; a job that only heard them doesn't. Another body under the key, or the same one signed differently (most often a byte-identical retry whose signed words were already used: sign the retry again), is `409`, and nothing is heard. A refused request (`400`, `403`, `404`, `422`) keeps no receipt. With no key, nothing here applies.
