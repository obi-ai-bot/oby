What. A job's worktree (and the build folders in it) stay on disk after its pull request merges, until someone deletes the job, which also deletes its record.

Why. Running many tasks fills the disk; the owner asked that every task clean its worktree at the end ("that part is very important").

How. When a job's pull request is merged or closed, or the job ends with nothing to publish, obid removes its worktree and its local branch by itself and keeps the job, its transcript and its result. It never frees a worktree that holds commits that aren't published or work that isn't committed. A failed job keeps its worktree but loses its ignored build folders (what git clean -Xd would remove). The job's JSON gains an add-only key saying the worktree is gone.

### Formal verification
A Kani harness over the pure decision may_free(merged_or_closed, unpublished_commits, uncommitted_work, running): never true while the job runs or anything unpublished or uncommitted remains. A Quint model docs/models/worktree_free.qnt with
// verify: main=worktree_free steps=12 invariants=neverLosesWork witnesses=neverFrees
where neverLosesWork says no state has a freed worktree and work that was never published.

Acceptance: new HOME scenarios with tests named after them; disk use after a merged job is back to what it was before the job, measured in the PR.
