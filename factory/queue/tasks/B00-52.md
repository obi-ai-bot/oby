The last of the simple factory's three tasks (B00-50, B00-51, B00-52). Sangam owns what the user sees: the canvas is a proposal, and his words lead. Where this file quotes words, they're the canvas's until he changes them.

## What
This is what a person sees of the switch and of `/factory`. Settings gets a Factory page holding one switch. While the switch is on, typing `/` in a project's conversation offers `/factory` first. Sending `/factory` with a task opens that project's factory beside the conversation, with the task already in it. Before a person turns the factory on, nothing about it shows anywhere but that one switch.

## Why
- **The owner, 2026-11-16:** "it should just be a toggle to turn it on or off and then we can just use "/" to send stuff to the factory … Simplify it as much as possible!" They chose this over a three-step setup sheet the same day.
- **A fresh Oby is a plain dispatcher.** Someone who never turns the factory on should never meet it, beyond the one switch in Settings.
- **Lane: dispatcher.** What the app shows of the dispatcher's own state, and one route it calls. The app runs nothing for an agent.

## How
1. **Settings › Factory.** A page `factory` between Connectors and About in `SETTINGS_PAGES` (`SettingsView.tsx`). It holds one group with one row: the label `Factory`, the line `Send a task to it with /factory in a project’s conversation.`, and a `Switch`. The switch reads and writes `factory` through `GET` and `PUT /v1/settings/jobs` (B00-50).
   - Under it while on: `Each project gets its own factory the first time you send it a task. Its tasks go: Build → Test → Review → Merge.`
   - While off: `Off: Oby hands each task to one agent, as it always has. Turning it off lets running tasks finish.`
   - B00-09 later adds each step's account and model under this switch, on the same page.
2. **The / menu (B00-04).** It shows `/factory` exactly when obid's `GET /v1/commands` lists it: on, and in a project (B00-51). The app adds no rule of its own. Its line is `Send this task to <project>’s factory: built, tested, reviewed, then merged`.
3. **Sending it.** `/factory` followed by words calls `POST /v1/projects/{id}/factory/run` with the conversation's project, the words, and a `clientRequestId` (R02). The sent message shows `/factory` as a token, as the canvas draws it. The project's factory then opens beside the conversation (A00-74), with the new job first.
4. **The first time.** When the answer says `"created": true`, the factory panel shows `<project> has a factory now, and this is its first task.` at its top until the panel is closed. The canvas draws this line in the conversation, as Oby's. Which place it goes is Sangam's call: the line itself isn't kept in obid's conversation.
5. **Refused.** A `409` `factory-off` (the switch was turned off on another of the owner's apps) shows obid's words under the message box and keeps the typed words in it.
6. **Off.** With the switch off, the button that shows the project's factory and `/factory` aren't shown. A running factory job's card stays in its conversation until it ends.
7. **Wire.** `apps/electron/src/shared/obid-wire.ts` gains `factory` on the jobs settings and on status, the run answer's `created`, a project's `factory`, and a job's `via`. All are optional, as obid's API only grows.
