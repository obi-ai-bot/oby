## What
- Once the computer button switches the window to another computer (COMPUTERSAPP-1 to 12), the window looks exactly as it does on This Mac. Only the computer button's own label changes, so a person can start a task, add an account or delete a conversation on the wrong Mac without noticing (seen by the owner on 2026-11-23 with their MacBook Pro paired).

## Why
Every action in the window lands on the computer in use: tasks run with that computer's agents and logins, files change on that computer's disk. Which Mac that is must be impossible to miss.

## How
1. While the computer in use isn't This Mac: a 2 px red frame (the app's danger red token, not a new colour) around the whole window, inside its rounded corners, over every screen (Home, conversations, Settings, sheets), drawn by one element at the window's root so no screen can forget it.
2. At the top of the window, centred in the title bar's drag area: "Running on <computer's name>" in the same red, small, with the computer's icon. A click opens the computer button's menu (switch back to This Mac). It never covers the traffic lights or the title bar's own controls.
3. While the computer is `lost` (no route answers): the same frame and line, with the line saying "<name> isn't answering". Nothing about This Mac changes.
4. Back on This Mac: no frame, no line.
5. The window's own chrome only: nothing in obid changes.

### Files
- apps/electron/src/renderer/src/computers/ (the current computer's state)
- the window root (App/Shell component) and app.css (the frame and the title-bar line)

Acceptance (tests named after new COMPUTERSAPP-n scenarios):
1. With another computer in use, the root has the frame and the title bar reads "Running on <name>"; on This Mac, neither.
2. The frame and the line show on Home, a conversation, Settings and an open sheet.
3. A lost computer shows "<name> isn't answering".
4. The line opens the computer menu; This Mac in it removes both.
5. Real Mac: pair a second Mac, switch to it, and the red frame and "Running on <name>" show at once; switch back, and they go.
