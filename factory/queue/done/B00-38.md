## What
`a_call_waits_for_the_reader_and_sees_the_read_before_it` (binary `conversation_harness`, core/crates/obid/tests/conversation_harness.rs, around line 1476, from #1689) ends with `assert!(at.elapsed() < Duration::from_secs(4))`: the call must come back in under 4 seconds, to show it didn't wait out obid's 5-second cap. On a loaded machine it took 4.29 s and more, so it fails on branches that don't touch it. B00-39 put it on the flaky list until this lands.

## Why
It failed A00-86's gate twice in a row (rounds 4 and 5, on the gate VM's busy Rust shard), on a branch that doesn't touch conversations, and pushed that job to its last round. A test that fails at random holds real work back from merging.

## How
Prove what the test means (the call's own line came, so it never waited out the cap) without a wall-clock bound that load can break: for example, make the cap in this test much longer than the bound, or check a signal that the reader answered rather than the elapsed time. Keep what the test proves; don't weaken it to always pass. Then remove its entry from the flaky list in `core/.config/nextest.toml` in the same change (docs/contracts/ci.md, CI-26 to 28).

Acceptance: the test passes 50 runs in a row on this Mac while another Rust build runs, it still fails if the call waits out the cap, and its flaky-list entry is gone.
