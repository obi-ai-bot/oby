## What
Board 6 draws the hold. The owner clicks Take control. The agent's next act is refused and its log row says so. The owner clicks Give back, and the agent goes on. The window already signs both clicks: `live.ts` posts an empty body to `POST /v1/browser/tabs/{id}/take` and `…/giveBack`. `docs/contracts/signed-words.md` says main signs them by the labels **Take control** and **Give back**. On `dev` both routes answer `410 retired` (`routes.rs`, the `retired` handler), so nothing is held.

The owner's decision D1 keeps board 6's hold and puts it in obid. This task brings back the two handlers. `input`, `secret` and `stream` stay retired.

## Why
Board 6 is how the owner gets a page back from an agent mid-task: to sign in, or to stop a wrong click. With the routes at 410, Take control does nothing, and the agent keeps clicking under the owner's hand. The window's half (board 6's strip, ring and dot, task B5) needs obid to hold first.

2. **A window in the test.** No Rust test on `dev` drives an agent tab through the relay. The relay's own unit tests (`browser::relay::tests`) show that obid sends no CDP to a tab until the window posts `attached`. So add a small stand-in window inside `tests/browser_api.rs`, not under `tests/support/` (a shared file).
   - It follows `GET /v1/browser/relay` with obid's runner token.
   - It posts `attached` for the job's new tab. It learns the tab from `GET /v1/browser` or `GET /v1/browser/events`.
